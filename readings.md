# Readings: Key Quotes for Motivation and Background

## 1. What Rhetorical Figures Are: Form/Function Alignments

> "Rhetorical figures are form/function alignments in which the form (1) serves to convey the function(s) and (2) supports their computational detection; therefore, (3) they are particularly rich for various text mining, argument analytics and NLU purposes."

— Harris (2023), p. 217 (Abstract)

---

> "The figures which are especially valuable in these ways are known as *schemes*, figures that are defined by their material (phonological, orthographical, morphological, or syntactic) form, in distinction particularly from *tropes*, which are defined by their conceptual (semantic) form."

— Harris (2023), p. 217 (Abstract)

---

> "Schemes, because they are entirely material, are computationally very tractable, so this article focuses on them almost exclusively."

— Harris (2023), p. 220

---

> "Rhetorical schemes are material figures, figures of form — figures like 'rhyme' and 'alliteration,' which work their effects because of lexically positioned phonological repetition (sounds repeat in specific locations within words), and like 'epanaphora' and 'epiphora,' which work their effects because of syntactically positioned lexical repetition (words repeat in specific locations within phrases or clauses). Because schemes are figures of form, they are a playground for what Charles Sanders Peirce called 'diagrammatic iconicity'."

— Harris (2022c), p. 104

---

> "Schematic forms enact schematic meanings."

— Harris (2022c), p. 104

---

## 2. Form Encodes Meaning: Cognitive and Epistemic Effects

> "We know empirically that people judge statements which include rhyme to be more accurate and credible than non-rhyming paraphrases (McGlone and Tofghbakhsh 1999, 2000; Filková and Klempe 2013). One could not ask for a clearer experimental confirmation that form-enacted meanings have rhetorical effects: lexical forms that resemble each other because they share syllables lead hearers to regard the associated meanings to be more compatible with each other than meanings associated with non-resembling (non-rhyming) lexical forms."

— Harris (2022c), p. 105

*[The rhyme-as-reason effect: empirical evidence that formal similarity induces epistemic trust.]*

---

> "When several parallel items are perceived in a series, the second or third items follow a path of construal prepared by the first. They satisfy an immediate expectation. Our minds are constructed to be receptive to repeated verbal or visual forms in any of their possible dimensions. It is not surprising, then, that items packaged in a similar form tend to be construed as equivalent. When an arguer's goal is to convince an audience that items are the same in some respect, then parallel form, verbal or visual, is an efficient choice."

— Fahnestock (2003), pp. 148

---

> "Hence, parallel form, whatever the content, is in itself a persuasive device."

— Fahnestock (2003), p. 147

---

> "Once a verbal form is available, like the possibility of casting multiple examples in parallel cola, that form itself can become generative. So it is not always the examples that determine the form of verbal expression; the verbal form can also determine what examples are selected or created. In other words, a syntactic formula for parallel instances, once established, can easily become the frame for further observations."

— Fahnestock (2003), pp. 136

---

## 3. Parallelism Epitomizes Argument (Fahnestock's Core Claim)

> "parallelism of sound, syntax, and semantics can epitomize an arguer's claim that multiple instances belong to the same grouping. The form, once established, can also dictate how subsequent instances are selected and expressed."

— Fahnestock (2003), p. 125

---

> "The form epitomizes the argument; it is the perfect expression of an induction based on examples."

— Fahnestock (2003), p. 132

---

> "A view of verbal and visual parallelism as the epitome of a type of argument recaptures an ancient conception of the figures of speech as formal bearers of meaning."

— Fahnestock (2003), p. 125

---

## 4. The Communicative Functions of Chiastic Structure

> "Chiastic structure (ABBA) leverages the iconicity principles of identity, sequential order, and quantity for consolidated iconicities of harmonious balance and cyclicity, effecting an aesthetic response that is very often but somewhat incongruously associated with mirror-image symmetry. The ABBA pattern does not effect this response alone, however. Chiastic figures collocate preferentially with other figures to achieve these effects, especially including figures of parallelism. These figural convergences, collocating additionally with certain grammatical features, raise the iconicity stakes somewhat further to enact a quite specific suite of meanings: COMPREHENSIVENESS, IRRELEVANCE OF ORDER, RECIPROCAL SPECIFICATION, and RECIPROCAL ENERGY."

— Harris (2022c), p. 103 (Abstract)

---

> "There is no rank or order, no material difference, between Rosencrantz and Guildenstern. The chiastic form, as it does in algebra and predicate calculus, conveys an IRRELEVANCE OF ORDER."

— Harris (2022c), p. 110

---

> "Whatever the situation is that (1) concerns, it is utterly and completely incorrigible because of mutual exclusion: having a solution negates comprehending the problem; comprehending the problem negates having a solution."

— Harris (2022c), p. 112

*[On the COMPREHENSIVENESS function: chiastic form enacts logical exhaustiveness.]*

---

> "The principle of sequential iconicity requires that the default syntactic order reflects the temporal order of the events it expresses. So there is no more elicit way to express the irrelevance of temporal order than to juxtapose two inverse orders, crystallized in the illustrative equation Boole highlights, x + y = y + x. It doesn't matter if you add x to y or add y to x. The order of operations is irrelevant. As Fahnestock notes, 'the commutative laws provide a blueprint for a particular class of arguments epitomized by the antimetabole'."

— Harris (2023), pp. 243–244

---

> "AMP constructs in natural language regularly express reciprocality... By changing position (antimetabole) in the same syntactic structure (parison) around the same mediating predicate (mesodiplosis), the relevant terms are put into a reciprocal relationship, either of energy flow or of identity-specification."

— Harris (2023), p. 244

---

## 5. The AMP Cluster: Parison, Mesodiplosis, Antimetabole

> "Antimetabole, mesodiplosis, and parison function so frequently together that we can refer to them collectively as AMP."

— Harris (2023), p. 240

---

> "Figural collocations of the sort we find in proverbs, aphorisms, and the many, many curated ABBA instances, are a highly neglected feature of rhetorical figurational studies more generally. Figures combine. But figural collocations are crucial for understanding iconicity, and the effects ascribed regularly to one single figure are very often the combined effects of figural convergence."

— Harris (2022c), pp. 122–123

---

## 6. The Computational Neglect Gap

> "Rhetorical schemes, however, have been almost universally ignored by linguists, including computational linguists."

— Harris (2023), p. 217

---

> "The computational benefits of distinctive formal patterns aligned with specific functions make that detection worthwhile because they can get at meaning in a way that is beyond current text mining and NLU tools."

— Harris (2023), p. 217 (Abstract)

---

> "So detecting figurative patterns and building a knowledge of their functions into NLU is essential for computational systems that hope to process language adequately."

— Harris (2023), p. 225

---

> "What is needed, if we are to incorporate rhetorical figures seriously into computational linguistics, is a programme that brings together the detection of rhetorical figures, in isolation but more importantly in collocations, with theories of (1) form/function alignments, and (2) Construction Grammar."

— Harris (2023), p. 240
